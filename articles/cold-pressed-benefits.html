Cold-Pressed Juice Benefits: What Matters and What Is Hype

Cold-pressed juice is genuinely different from blended or heat-treated juice — lower heat during extraction, often better yield from the same produce, and shorter, refrigeration-dependent shelf life. Here is the honest version of what that means.

How Cold-Pressing Differs

The core difference is process-driven: cold-pressing introduces less heat during extraction than high-speed blade systems. Less heat and less oxidation exposure means heat-sensitive compounds and fresh flavour are better preserved.

The other practical difference is yield. Cold-pressing generally extracts more usable juice from the same quantity of produce than a typical home blender or juicer.

What This Means for Customers

Taste stability: flavors stay cleaner when oxidation and heat stress are lower.

Short shelf behavior: quality tends to hold more consistently in the first refrigeration window.

Ingredient efficiency: better extraction means more value from each kilogram of produce.

🔬

The Honest Takeaway

Our reason to trust our process is the method itself — cold-pressed and ozone-washed — not exaggerated health claims. We would rather under-promise and make a good juice.

Why Ozonated Water Still Matters

Cold-pressed does not sterilize produce by itself. If the ingredient is raw, surface hygiene matters before pressing. That is why every Glengala ingredient is cleaned in ozonated water before extraction.
